STAGE-5B -- EVIDENCE FOR THE fill_ratio ESTIMATOR DEFECT
========================================================================

This is the retained evidence from the FIRST Stage-5B recording, made
before the fill_ratio clamp documented in obstacle_classification.hpp was
applied. The full raw JSONL (2.2 MB) was not kept; this summary is the
part that matters. The reported Stage-5B metrics come from a SEPARATE,
clean re-recording made after the fix -- not from this data.

THE DEFECT
------------------------------------------------------------------------
fill_ratio is defined as the fraction of the beams crossing a cluster that
returned, so it cannot exceed 1. It was computed as
    point_count / (angular_span / angle_increment + 1)
and in practice exceeded 1 on a third of all detections:

  detections            : 2816
  min / median / max    : 0.429 / 1.011 / 1.375
  above 1.00            : 1425 (50.6%)
  above 1.05 (the bound : 977 (34.7%)
    every class used)

ROOT CAUSE, CONFIRMED NUMERICALLY
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The angular span is measured from the returns own bearings, while
angle_increment is the driver nominal spacing. This LiDAR reports 360
samples over 6.28 rad rather than 2*pi, so its first and last beams are
2*pi - 6.28 = 0.0032 rad apart -- 0.18 of one 0.01749 rad increment. A
cluster straddling that direction (anything straight ahead of the sensor,
which is where these actors are) therefore contributes one extra return
while adding only 0.18 increments of span, so expected_beams is deflated
by ~0.82 beams and the quotient rises above 1.

Predicted span deficit from that mechanism : 1 - 0.18 = 0.82 increments
Measured, as (point_count - 1) - angular_span/angle_increment :

    points       n   median span/inc   (n-1)   deficit  median fill
         3      42              3.19       2     -1.19         0.72
         4     256              2.19       3      0.81         1.26
         5     180              6.01       4     -2.01         0.71
         6     238              4.19       5      0.81         1.16
         7      98              6.01       6     -0.01         1.00
         8     220              6.20       7      0.80         1.11
         9     163              8.02       8     -0.02         1.00
        10     248              8.20       9      0.80         1.09
        11     106             10.02      10     -0.02         1.00
        12     104             10.21      11      0.79         1.07

Reading the table: a POSITIVE deficit means the measured span is SHORTER
than (n-1) increments, which is geometrically impossible for n distinct
beams at the nominal spacing -- it can only happen if two returns sit at
nearly the same bearing. Every such row sits at 0.79-0.81, matching the
0.82 the near-duplicate beam pair predicts, and every one of those rows
reports a median fill_ratio above 1.

The NEGATIVE rows are the correct, healthy case: those clusters span more
increments than they have returns because they have genuine internal holes
(a pedestrian between its legs, a bicycle between its wheels), beams that
crossed the silhouette and returned nothing near it. Those report
fill_ratio at or below 1, as the definition requires.

CONSEQUENCE
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every class box bounds fill_ratio from above (fill_hi 0.90-1.05). An
estimator artefact was therefore being read as "this object is impossibly
solid", and the affected detections were refused a label. Labels assigned
in this pre-fix recording, for reference:

  true PEDESTRIAN  n=574   UNKNOWN:314  PEDESTRIAN:260
  true BIKE        n=582   PEDESTRIAN:551  UNKNOWN:31
  true VEHICLE     n=993   UNKNOWN:443  VEHICLE:337  PEDESTRIAN:213
  true UNKNOWN     n=667   UNKNOWN:500  VEHICLE:167

THE FIX
------------------------------------------------------------------------
fill_ratio is clamped to 1.0, which is its definitional maximum. The
clamp and this measurement are documented at ClusterFeatures::fill_ratio,
and pinned by a unit test that sweeps every actor geometry, aspect and
range and requires fill_ratio <= 1.0 throughout.
