# Stage-4F validation evidence

Wide-arena predictive-navigation benchmark: reactive vs predictive across four
dynamic-obstacle scenario classes and three obstacle speeds, 10 + 10 trials per
condition.

Extends, and does not modify, the Stage-4B/4C/4D/4E evidence. The tracking
algorithm, the Kalman model, the predictive-cost mathematics and the Stage-4D
and Stage-4E parameter files are **byte-identical** to commit `49ce50d`
(verified: `git diff 49ce50d -- src/predictive_nav_costmap src/predictive_nav_tracking`
is empty), so Stage-4E remains reproducible. Stage-4F adds a world, configs,
scenario tooling, evaluation, plots and documentation only.

**Result: Stage-4F PASSES**, with one regression item failing and fully
diagnosed (section 10), and one qualified finding from the controller-weight
ablation (section 8).

---

## 1. What Stage-4E left open, and what Stage-4F answers

Stage-4E ran in `tb3_sandbox`, whose corridors are 0.70 m wide. Two things were
unresolved:

1. Reactive navigation succeeded in only 2/10 trials. Was that a property of
   prediction-less navigation, or of the world?
2. The corridor had no room for a lateral manoeuvre, so prediction could only
   ever express itself as a stop.

Stage-4F answers both: **in a wide arena the reactive baseline succeeds
10/10 in every scenario class** (so Stage-4E's 2/10 was a world constraint),
and prediction produces genuine lateral detours where the geometry rewards
them. The predictive advantage survives, but it is now an advantage in
*margin and timing* rather than in *success or failure* — which is the more
convincing result.

## 2. Benchmark arena

`worlds/stage4f_benchmark.sdf.xacro` and `maps/stage4f_benchmark.yaml` are both
generated by `scripts/make_stage4f_world.py` from one `GEOMETRY` list, so the
simulated room and the map the robot localises against cannot drift apart. Both
artefacts are committed; the generator is idempotent.

| Element | Geometry |
|---|---|
| Interior | x ∈ [-4.93, 4.93], y ∈ [-3.93, 3.93] -- **10 × 8 m clear** |
| Walls | 0.15 m thick, 0.6 m tall (LiDAR sits at z ≈ 0.18 m) |
| Pillars | 0.30 m squares at (-3.0, 3.0), (1.2, 3.0), (-1.5, -3.0), (2.5, -3.0) |
| Robot route | straight, y = 0, from **(-3.0, 0)** to **(3.0, 0)**, 6.0 m |
| Conflict point | **(0, 0)** (head-on: (-0.40, 0), where the two actually meet) |

The pillars exist only to keep AMCL out of a degenerate plain rectangle. They
are deliberately asymmetric and sit off every scenario path: ≥ 1.2 m from the
obstacle's crossing column, ≥ 1.0 m from the oblique diagonal, and far enough
from the route that their inflation stops at |y| = 2.15.

### The §2 quantitative check, before any experiment

Clearance to the nearest static obstacle along the route is **1.40-3.02 m**, so
with `inflation_radius: 0.70` the entire route and a ±2.3 m band around it
carry **zero** static inflation cost. Measured on `/local_costmap/costmap_raw`
in the strip the MPPI controller can actually reach, over 3 obstacle-free runs:

| | Stage-4F arena | Stage-4E tb3_sandbox |
|---|---|---|
| Peak master cost ahead | **0** | 253 |
| Mean master cost ahead | **0.0** | 186.0 |
| Cells ≥ 180 ahead | **0** | ~129 |

The route is not merely "less saturated" -- the controller's reachable band is
**completely empty** obstacle-free. Predicted occupancy is therefore the only
thing in it, is fully distinguishable, and there is 2.3 m of zero-cost space on
either side for the controller to steer into.

## 3. Configuration

### Controller -- identical in both arms

`nav2_mppi_controller::MPPIController`, 56 × 0.05 s = 2.8 s horizon, stock
critics, **`CostCritic.cost_weight: 12.0`** carried over unchanged from
Stage-4E. No custom critic, no Nav2 source change. Section 8 ablates this
weight against Nav2's stock 3.81.

### Differences from `nav2_stage4e_params.yaml`

Four values, all in the **local** costmap, all applied identically to both arms:

| Change | From | To | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| local costmap width/height | 3 m | **6 m** | The arena is wide enough for a real manoeuvre, but a 3 m window shows the controller only ±1.5 m of it and would clip the predicted sweep of a 0.75 m/s obstacle (0.75 × 3.0 s = 2.25 m). A prediction painted outside the rolling window is invisible (Stage-4D limitation 1). |
| voxel `obstacle_max_range` | 2.5 m | **3.0 m** | So ordinary reactive sensing covers the *whole* enlarged window. |
| voxel `raytrace_max_range` | 3.0 m | **3.5 m** | As above. |
| `predicted_obstacle_layer.min_cost` | 200 | **0** | The one predictive-policy change; see below. |

The two sensing-range changes deliberately **strengthen the reactive arm**.
Without them the predictive arm would enjoy extra spatial reach as well as
temporal lead, and part of any advantage would be a sensor-range artefact. With
them, prediction's only advantage is time-extrapolation of observations that
are themselves inside reactive sensing range. The *global* costmap's obstacle
layer is left at the Stage-4D/4E values, so global planning is unchanged.

### Frozen Stage-4F predictive policy

**One** value differs from the validated Stage-4E policy. Used for **every**
primary predictive trial, every scenario, every obstacle speed -- never tuned
per scenario.

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `max_prediction_horizon` | **3.0 s** | Stage-4C publishes to exactly 3.0 s; MPPI integrates 2.8 s |
| `sigma_level` | **1.5** | ~67% of 2D probability mass |
| `temporal_decay` | **0.35** | monotonic, 0.84 at 0.5 s → 0.35 at 3.0 s |
| `max_cost` | **250** | < `MAX_NON_OBSTACLE` (252) and < 253, which CostCritic treats as a collision |
| `min_cost` | **0** ← *the one change* (was 200) | see below |
| `max_influence_radius` | **0.55 m** | robot radius 0.22 + obstacle radius 0.20 + 0.13 margin |

`min_cost` was 200 in Stage-4E for a specific measured reason: the robot's own
centreline there already carried 88-170 of static inflation, so anything below
that floor was invisible under `updateWithMax`. **That reason does not exist
here** -- section 2 measures the route's ambient cost as exactly zero. Keeping
the 200 floor would make the predicted region a hard-edged uniform block;
restoring `min_cost: 0` restores Stage-4D's original rationale (a cell at the
ellipse boundary costs nothing, a true no-op under max-combination) and gives
MPPI a smooth cost *shape* to steer around rather than a wall to stop at. That
gradient is what makes a lateral manoeuvre expressible, which is the point of
Stage-4F.

### Mode switch

Both arms load the identical params file, plugin set, tracker, world, map,
start, goal, obstacle schedule and robot limits. The trial runner sets exactly
one parameter differently before sending the goal:

```
/local_costmap/local_costmap  predicted_obstacle_layer.enabled  =  False | True
```

Trials are interleaved (R, P, R, P, …) and each runs on its own
`ROS_DOMAIN_ID` after a verified process teardown.

## 4. Scenarios and the obstacle-release rule

Obstacle: the same 0.20 m radius, 0.6 m tall cylinder used since Stage-4B,
driven one-way along a fixed heading, detected **only** from simulated LiDAR.

Every conflict scenario spawns it **3.0 m from the conflict point** and
releases it at

```
t_release = T_ROBOT_TO_CONFLICT - ARRIVAL_OFFSET - 3.0 / v
          = 7.07 - 1.00 - 3.0 / v
```

so the obstacle reaches the conflict point a fixed 1.0 s ahead of the robot
whatever its speed. `T_ROBOT_TO_CONFLICT = 7.07 s` is measured from 3
obstacle-free runs (7.05 / 7.05 / 7.10, sd 0.02). At 0.25 m/s the release time
is negative, i.e. the obstacle is already under way when the goal is issued;
the runner handles that as a pre-roll. Spawn geometry is therefore fixed and
**speed is the only variable** across the speed study.

### Why a 1.0 s offset and not simultaneous arrival

With exact simultaneous arrival a 0.49 m/s robot **cannot geometrically avoid**
a 0.5 m/s crosser: a perpendicular crosser is not on the robot's path until the
last moment (the bodies conflict only inside 0.42 m), so reactive sensing gets
no usable warning. Measured: the reactive arm collided at -0.20 m penetration
and never recovered. Benchmarking against a baseline that is doomed by
construction proves nothing, and §12 explicitly warns against it.

For a robot that does **not** react, closest approach is
`v_robot · v_obs · offset / sqrt(v_robot² + v_obs²)`:

| offset | 0.25 m/s | 0.50 m/s | 0.75 m/s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 s | **0.22 m** | **0.35 m** | **0.41 m** |

All three are below the 0.42 m contact threshold, so at 1.0 s the conflict is
real and the robot is genuinely forced to act at every speed -- but a modest
brake or a ~0.2 m lateral shift clears it, so a late-reacting reactive baseline
can still succeed. That is the fair comparison §12 asks for.

| Condition | Class | Obstacle start | Heading | Speed | Release | Robot vs obstacle time-to-conflict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `perp_025` | A perpendicular | (0.0, -3.0) | +90° | 0.25 m/s | **-5.93 s** (pre-roll) | 7.07 s vs 6.07 s |
| `perp_050` | A perpendicular | (0.0, -3.0) | +90° | 0.50 m/s | +0.07 s | 7.07 s vs 6.07 s |
| `perp_075` | A perpendicular | (0.0, -3.0) | +90° | 0.75 m/s | +2.07 s | 7.07 s vs 6.07 s |
| `oblique` | B oblique | (-2.12, -2.12) | +45° | 0.50 m/s | +0.07 s | 7.07 s vs 6.07 s |
| `headon` | C head-on | (3.0, 0.25) | 180° | 0.50 m/s | 0.0 s | closes at 0.96 m/s, meets near x = -0.4 |
| `noconflict` | D control | (3.0, 1.5) | 180° | 0.50 m/s | 0.0 s | parallel lane 1.5 m off-route; never intersects |
| `nominal` | baseline | *(no obstacle spawned)* | -- | -- | -- | defines thresholds and `T_ROBOT_TO_CONFLICT` |

Head-on uses a +0.25 m lane offset: the obstacle passes *through* the robot's
corridor (0.25 m < the 0.42 m contact threshold, so doing nothing collides) but
a ~0.2 m lateral shift clears it, and there is 2.3 m of zero-cost space to do
it in. The no-conflict control keeps the obstacle continuously visible,
tracked and predicted 1.5 m off-route, so it guards against the layer acting as
a generic penalty field rather than a prediction of a specific conflict.

### Reaction thresholds, frozen before the benchmark

From 3 obstacle-free runs in this arena: commanded cruise median 0.491 m/s
(min 0.483), |ω| ≤ 0.088 rad/s, lateral offset from the nominal route
≤ 0.057 m. Every threshold sits well outside that envelope.

| Event | Definition | Nominal envelope |
|---|---|---|
| **R1 primary** | commanded vₓ < 0.288 m/s, sustained 0.25 s | never below 0.483 |
| R2 | \|ω\| > 0.30 rad/s, sustained 0.25 s | max 0.088 |
| R3 | \|offset from route\| > 0.15 m, sustained 0.25 s | max 0.057 |
| R4 | commanded vₓ < 0.05 m/s, sustained 0.25 s | never |

Scoring opens only once the robot reaches cruise (the first ~1.3 s is the
startup ramp, below threshold in *every* run including obstacle-free ones) and
only while the robot is still approaching the conflict point. Reaction is
scored on `/cmd_vel_nav`, the **controller's own output**, upstream of the
velocity smoother and collision monitor, so "Nav2 reacted" means the controller
reacted and not a downstream safety filter.

### Safety

* **Success is 10/10 in every condition, in both arms.** Stage-4E's 2/10
  reactive success was a world constraint, confirmed.
* **Collisions: 6 (all `headon` reactive) → 0 predictive.** The reactive arm's
  worst head-on case penetrates 0.155 m.
* Mean minimum clearance improves in every conflict condition, by 5-6× at the
  two higher crossing speeds (0.133 → 0.795 m and 0.159 → 0.783 m). Cohen's *d*
  is 20-25 for those, and the 95% CIs do not overlap
  (perp_050: reactive [0.109, 0.157], predictive [0.783, 0.808]).
* **Collision-monitor interventions: 1.0-1.7 per reactive trial → 0.000 in
  every predictive trial of every conflict condition.** The predictive arm
  never needed the emergency safety layer at all.

### Efficiency -- the cost of prediction is ~nil

Navigation time differs by **+0.5 s at worst** (18.18 → 18.64 s on perp_050,
+2.5%), and the predictive arm is *faster* on perp_025 (-1.24 s) and on headon
(-1.88 s), where the reactive arm's late braking costs more than the predictive
arm's early yield. Predictive **path length is consistently shorter** on the
crossing scenarios (6.58 → 5.80 m, 6.50 → 5.74 m, 6.47 → 5.88 m) because
reacting early means a smoother, less corrective trajectory. Predictive stop
duration is lower in every conflict condition.

### Reaction and trajectory behaviour

Predictive reacts earlier in **every** conflict condition, by 0.76-2.92 s, with
Cohen's *d* from -6.2 to -17.6. The reactive arm's `min_v_nav_approach` reaches
-0.35 m/s (hard reverse) on perp_050/075, versus -0.03 to -0.07 m/s predictive:
reactive brakes and backs up, predictive modulates.

**Response classification** (§9) shows the wide arena delivering what Stage-4E
could not:

| condition | reactive | predictive |
|---|---|---|
| perp_050 / perp_075 | stop_yield 10 | stop_yield 10 |
| oblique | stop_yield 9, lateral+yield 1 | **lateral_detour+yield 6**, stop_yield 4 |
| headon | none 2, stop_yield 3, lateral+yield 5 | **lateral_detour+yield 9, lateral_detour 1** |
| noconflict | none 10 | none 10 |

On head-on the predictive arm reaches a 0.446 m lateral offset versus 0.318 m
reactive, and on oblique 0.353 m versus 0.177 m with integrated |lateral| of
1.69 vs 0.66 m·s -- a sustained detour, not a wobble. So §18.10 is satisfied:
behaviour beyond forced emergency stopping does occur, where geometry rewards
it. On the pure perpendicular crossings both arms still yield, which is the
correct response to something crossing directly in front.

### Advance warning

Time between predictive cost first occupying the conflict point and the
obstacle physically arriving there: **2.80-4.69 s** depending on condition
(perp_025 4.69 ± 0.02, perp_050 4.20 ± 0.14, perp_075 2.80 ± 0.11,
oblique 4.35 ± 0.09, headon 4.65 ± 0.12), in 10/10 trials of every condition.

## 6. Obstacle-speed study

The most interpretable result in Stage-4F.

| obstacle speed | reactive clearance | predictive clearance | ratio | reaction lead |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 m/s | 0.584 m | 0.664 m | 1.14× | 0.76 s |
| 0.50 m/s | **0.133 m** | 0.795 m | **6.0×** | 1.90 s |
| 0.75 m/s | **0.159 m** | 0.783 m | **4.9×** | 1.93 s |

**Reactive clearance collapses as the obstacle speeds up; predictive clearance
does not.** At 0.25 m/s the obstacle takes 3.6 s to cross the 0.90 m band where
ordinary sensing plus inflation can see it, which is ample warning, and the
reactive baseline does nearly as well as the predictive one. At 0.50-0.75 m/s
that warning shrinks to 1.8-1.2 s and reactive clearance falls by a factor of
four, while the predictive arm holds ~0.78-0.80 m regardless.

This is the expected structure: reactive warning is a fixed *distance*
(obstacle radius + inflation radius), so its warning *time* is inversely
proportional to obstacle speed, whereas predictive warning is a fixed *time*
(the prediction horizon). Reaction lead rises with speed and then saturates
near 1.9 s, bounded by how early the predicted corridor can enter the
controller's own 2.8 s reachable set.

## 7. Prediction quality

Scored against Gazebo ground truth, which is used for evaluation only.
For a prediction issued at *t* with horizon *h*, the reference is the
obstacle's true position at *t + h*, linearly interpolated from the 20 Hz
ground-truth series; predictions whose reference falls outside the recorded
window are skipped rather than clamped.

| condition | tracking err [m] | velocity err [m/s] | ADE [m] | FDE [m] | 0.5 s | 1.0 s | 2.0 s | 3.0 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| perp_025 | 0.198 | 0.039 | 0.209 | 0.231 | 0.198 | 0.199 | 0.212 | 0.238 |
| perp_050 | 0.195 | 0.059 | 0.256 | 0.340 | 0.195 | 0.210 | 0.269 | 0.364 |
| perp_075 | 0.193 | 0.073 | 0.336 | 0.535 | 0.196 | 0.227 | 0.366 | 0.595 |
| oblique | 0.197 | 0.058 | 0.257 | 0.342 | 0.199 | 0.212 | 0.272 | 0.369 |
| headon | 0.166 | 0.081 | 0.270 | 0.397 | 0.171 | 0.200 | 0.293 | 0.435 |
| noconflict | 0.167 | 0.081 | 0.248 | 0.358 | 0.170 | 0.196 | 0.276 | 0.343 |

The **~0.19 m floor at h = 0.5 s is not prediction error** -- it is the
Stage-4B/4C LiDAR-surface-versus-centre bias, already documented at 0.149 m in
Stage-4B and expected to be close to the 0.20 m cylinder radius, since the
tracker centroids the visible surface rather than the model's geometric centre.
Genuine prediction growth is the part above that floor: at 3.0 s it is
0.04 m (0.25 m/s), 0.17 m (0.50 m/s) and 0.40 m (0.75 m/s), i.e. error grows
roughly linearly with obstacle speed, as a constant-velocity model should.

This closes the chain the brief asks for: **prediction quality (FDE 0.23-0.60 m
at 3 s) → predictive cost (≈1000-1200 cells, 7-8% of the local window) →
navigation outcome (5-6× clearance, 0 collisions).** Note the predictive region
occupies only **7.4% of the local window** on perp_050 -- compact, not a
blanket, which is what lets the controller still choose a route through it.

## 8. Controller-weight ablation (§13) -- kept separate

Same predictive policy, same scenario (`perp_050`), 10 + 10 trials, changing
only `CostCritic.cost_weight` via `config/nav2_stage4f_params_stockcritic.yaml`.

| CostCritic weight | reactive clr | predictive clr | worst P | **reaction lead** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **12.0** (Stage-4E, primary) | 0.133 m | **0.795 m** | 0.768 | **1.90 s** |
| **3.81** (Nav2 stock) | 0.181 m | 0.444 m | 0.114 | **0.04 s** |

Both weights: 10/10 success, 0 collisions, in both arms.

**The answer is "partly".** Predictive information improves the mean clearance
even at Nav2's stock weight -- 0.181 → 0.444 m, a 2.5× improvement -- so the
benefit is not purely an artefact of aggressive cost weighting. But the
*earlier reaction* essentially disappears at stock weight (lead 0.04 s), and
the worst-case predictive clearance falls to 0.114 m, barely better than
reactive's 0.097 m. So at stock weight the predictive layer shifts the average
outcome without reliably changing *when* the controller acts.

Read honestly: prediction supplies the information, and CostCritic's weight
determines how much authority that information has over the controller. Neither
alone produces the Stage-4F headline result. This is reported as a dependency of
the result, not hidden.

## 10. Failure analysis (§14) and regression (§17)

### Failure register -- all retained, none discarded

6 failures across 120 trials, **all in the `headon` reactive arm**, all
classified `collision_monitor_intervention`: the robot reacted too late, the
collision monitor fired (0.8 ± 0.6 interventions per trial), and body contact
occurred anyway (-0.026 to -0.155 m). Every one still reached the goal, so they
appear as `SUCCEEDED` with `collision: true` -- which is why collisions are
reported separately from success throughout. Full state at the moment of
failure (robot pose, obstacle pose, tracked state, prediction, predictive cost,
cmd_vel, collision-monitor count, tracked-frame fraction) is captured per trial
in `stage4f_trials.csv` and the per-trial JSON. **Zero failures in any
predictive trial of any condition.**

### The one failing regression, diagnosed

In the Stage-4F arena the predictive layer **does not always stay at zero cells
in an obstacle-free world**, which it did in Stage-4D's tb3_sandbox test B
(0 cells in 220/220 frames).

Measured over 10 valid obstacle-free runs with the layer enabled:
**6/10 runs produced spurious predictive cells**, of 1, 29, 152, 180, 377 and
441 cells (441 = 3.1% of the 14400-cell window).

Cause, established rather than assumed. Logging every published track in an
obstacle-free arena (`track_log` in the series files) shows every spurious
track sits **0.11-0.14 m from a mapped wall cell, at 3.7-7.3 m range from the
robot** (median 6.1 m), with fabricated speeds of 0.08-1.05 m/s. The mechanism:

* Stage-4B's static-rejection filter uses a **fixed** `static_reject_radius`
  of 0.15 m against the map.
* The LiDAR has 360 samples over 360°, so adjacent beams are 1° apart --
  0.035 m at 2 m range, but **0.105 m at 6 m**.
* In `tb3_sandbox` the walls were never more than ~2.5 m away, so scan returns
  landed comfortably inside the 0.15 m rejection radius. This arena is
  10 × 8 m, so returns from the far walls scatter to 0.15-0.20 m from the
  mapped wall under normal AMCL pose error, survive rejection, cluster
  trivially (`cluster_distance` 0.35 m ≫ 0.105 m beam spacing), and reach
  `cluster_min_points: 3`.
* A false track with a fabricated ~1 m/s velocity projects a 3 s prediction up
  to 3 m, which can reach into the robot's 6 × 6 m window even though the track
  itself is by a distant wall.

This is the Stage-4B limitation already recorded in `STAGE4D_README.md`
("static rejection remains sensitive to localization error"), now quantified
and shown to be **scale-dependent**, not localisation-error-dependent alone.

**Impact: none measurable on navigation.** All 10 obstacle-free runs succeeded
in 13.25-13.50 s against a reactive baseline of 13.30-13.41 s; maximum lateral
offset was 0.070 m against a nominal envelope of 0.057 m. **It also cannot bias
the A/B comparison**, because the tracker runs identically in both arms -- false
tracks appear in reactive trials too (e.g. `headon_reactive_04` saw 12 tracks);
only the predictive arm converts them into cost, and the obstacle-free
measurement bounds that contribution at ≤ 3.1% of the window versus the
~7-8% the real obstacle produces.

It was **not fixed**, because the brief protects the Stage-4B tracker from
redesign and because the defect demonstrably did not affect any benchmark
conclusion. The fix belongs in Stage-4B and is straightforward: scale
`static_reject_radius` with measurement range (e.g. `0.15 + range · beam_angle`)
or gate track creation by range. Recommended as the first item of any Stage-4G.

## 14. Known limitations

1. **Obstacle-free predictive occupancy is no longer exactly zero** in this
   larger arena -- 6/10 runs, up to 3.1% of the window. Diagnosed in section 10;
   no measurable navigation impact, cannot bias the A/B, not fixed because the
   fix belongs in the protected Stage-4B tracker.
2. **The result is partly dependent on the CostCritic reweight.** Section 8:
   mean clearance still improves 2.5× at Nav2's stock weight, but the earlier
   reaction does not survive it.
3. **MPPI scores a time-agnostic costmap snapshot.** The layer paints the whole
   swept future corridor at once, so the controller cannot reason "I will be
   there at t+1 s when the obstacle is elsewhere" and cannot deliberately pass
   *behind* a crosser. On pure perpendicular crossings the response is
   therefore always yield, never "slip through behind". Time-parameterised
   costing would need a custom critic, which Stage-4F deliberately did not write.
4. **One obstacle, one size, one shape.** Scenario E (two independent
   obstacles) was not run: the core four were the priority and the brief
   defers E until they pass. The tracker supports multiple tracks and the layer
   sums over them, so this is a runtime cost, not a design gap.
5. **Repeated trials of a deterministic simulator are not independent samples.**
   Cohen's *d* and the CIs describe the separation between arms under
   startup/timing jitter; they are not population inferences. No p-values are
   reported and none should be inferred.
6. **The arrival offset is a design choice.** 1.0 s was selected because it
   forces a reaction at all three speeds while leaving the reactive baseline a
   genuine chance (section 4). A smaller offset makes reactive fail by
   construction; a larger one makes the conflict trivial. The sensitivity of
   the headline numbers to this choice was not swept.
7. **Prediction quality is measured against a constant-velocity ground truth.**
   The scripted obstacle moves at constant velocity, which is exactly the
   Stage-4C model's assumption, so the ADE/FDE figures are a best case for this
   filter. Manoeuvring obstacles would degrade them.
